How to play

how to play quick start

  1. Move. Tap WAKE UP, then push the MOVE stick (or WASD, or swipe the screen). Moving is playing: the ground picks the melodies, and walls, objects and seeds ring when you touch them.
  2. Explore. Each world starts in darkness and is uncovered as you travel; high ground lets you see further. Lights and sounds carry through the dark, and first sightings are announced. Your map is saved per world (fog on/off and % explored are in the pause menu).
  3. Wander. WANDER (key 5) hands over to the autopilot. It heads for the edge of the unexplored dark, stops to look at things that catch its eye (landmarks, camps, animals, town lights at night), and finds its way around mountains and water. Touch any control to take back over.
  4. The band. HUM, PAD, KEYS and SLOT 4 (empty for now). Tap a part to switch it on and select it; tap again for the next instrument you own; hold to switch it off (keys 1–4, Shift+1–4). GLOBAL selects no part, so its faders move every part at once.
  5. Faders. Voice faders shape the selected part; each part has a gold LEVEL. On GLOBAL, LEVEL is the overall volume (it starts in the middle) and AMBIENCE sets the world sounds. Master faders: space, echo, tape drift, drive, and SAT (master tape saturation). TV view (pause menu): only the world, full screen, wandering, for casting your phone to a TV; tap for EXIT. Mode switch above them: MAN = only your settings; WORLD = your settings plus weather and places; SEMI = the automatic drift (default); AUTO = Drift plays the whole console for you (everything except the engine), in slow moods like WASH, GLASS, HAZE, DEEP, BLOOM and SPARSE; touch a fader and it leaves that one alone for a minute; leaving AUTO puts your settings back. Double-tap a fader to reset it. Dashed ghost caps show where your movement is pushing the sound.
  6. ARP. Press the lit button to run the arpeggiator on your pad (the red lamp shows it's on). RATE sets the speed; the button below changes the pattern.
  7. Arrow pad. ▲▼ change chord for the whole band, whichever part is selected. ◀▶ choose the scale. OCT −/+ (or [ ]) shift KEYS or PAD an octave. KEY moves the piece up a fifth; hold for down.
  8. Scales & chords. Eight scales, from lydian to whole tone. Chords follow the scale and the ground beneath you; the chord shows top left. Now and then everything shifts together with a swell.
  9. OK button. Beside a train, cart, docked ship or balloon, press OK (or Enter) to ride, and again to hop off. At a town or city OK opens the shop (towns sell instruments, cities synths; each part holds 3). Beside a busker it tips a note.
  10. Notes & power-ups. Collect gold notes (you start with 10). Power-ups wait in the tray: tap to use.
  11. The world ages. It starts wild with no people; nomadic tribes wander in, camp and some settle. Then come settlement, the medieval age (castles, a tavern by night), industry (cars, ships, planes, trains) and ruins, and it goes wild again. Wildlife roams the wild land, climates swing wet and dry, burnt forest regrows, and comets, eclipses and auroras take over the harmony. Visit a camp to hear its fire music; stand still and your band joins in. Bump a mountain three times to dig; dig far enough and you may find the deep.
  12. Pause. PAUSE (or Esc) freezes everything and opens the menu: new world and size, age cycle length, fog of war, fresh start, sleep timer, light or dark, full screen and this guide.
  13. Background. Switch apps or turn the screen off and Drift keeps playing, with wander exploring for you; play, pause and next (a chord shift) appear on the lock screen. Turn it off in the pause menu. Works best in the installed app.

The world is a generated landscape of oceans, rivers, forests, mountains, roads, towns and cities. Towns grow into cities, cities fall into ruins, and new ones are founded. Each day is a season, and the weather rolls in. Walls are an instrument: each hit plays the next note of an in-key melody. Type a name under WORLD to grow a world from it: the same name always gives the same map, key and scale, and the link in your address bar shares it.
